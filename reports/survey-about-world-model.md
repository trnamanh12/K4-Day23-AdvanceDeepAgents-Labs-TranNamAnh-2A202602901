# A Comprehensive Survey of World Models in Deep Learning and Embodied AI

## TL;DR
World models have emerged as a foundational paradigm in artificial intelligence, bridging sensory perception, generative simulation, and closed-loop decision-making. By internally modeling environment dynamics, physical laws, and action-conditioned transitions, modern world models enable reinforcement learning agents, generative video simulators, and robotic systems to imagine futures, plan trajectories, and evaluate policies offline. This survey synthesizes recent breakthroughs across recurrent state-space models, diffusion-based world simulators, latent action models, and self-supervised joint-embedding architectures. Furthermore, we examine emerging evaluation benchmarks in autonomous driving and robotics, analyzing critical open challenges such as compounding roll-out errors, optimism bias, and the gap between open-loop visual realism and closed-loop control reliability.

## Background
The concept of a world model—an internal representation of how an environment operates—has deep roots in cognitive science and control theory. In machine learning, early formulations utilized tabular models or parametric transition functions for model-based reinforcement learning. As high-dimensional sensory streams such as video and LiDAR became central to AI, modern world models evolved to compress raw inputs into compact latent spaces while predicting future states conditioned on agent actions. 

Architecturally, world models depart from purely reactive policies by simulating environment dynamics. By generating anticipated trajectories internally, agents can perform imagination-based planning, evaluate counterfactual decisions without interacting with the physical environment, and regularize offline reinforcement learning [1][2]. Recent scaling trends have expanded world models from task-specific recurrent networks to massive video generative models, foundation interactive environments, and self-supervised predictive architectures [3][4][5].

## Foundations and Architectural Paradigms
Model-based reinforcement learning relies heavily on recurrent state-space models (RSSMs) to handle partially observable environments and complex sensory dynamics. For example, DreamerV3 established a general and scalable algorithm capable of mastering over 150 diverse tasks using a single hyperparameter configuration [1]. The RSSM architecture compresses sensory inputs into stochastic latent representations via an encoder, predicts temporal sequences using a recurrent state model conditioned on past actions, and jointly optimizes dynamics, reward, and continuation predictors [1].

Beyond recurrent architectures, recent advances explore direct multi-step future prediction to mitigate the compounding errors inherent in recursive step-by-step rollouts. The Diffusion World Model (DWM) framework predicts multi-step future states and rewards simultaneously in a single forward pass, integrating into value estimation via Diffusion Model Based Value Expansion [2]. To enhance long-term memory and linear-time sequence modeling, unified architectures like EDELINE combine state space models with diffusion models, outperforming baseline models in memory-demanding environments such as Crafter and ViZDoom [6].

## Generative Video Models and Interactive Simulation
Large-scale video generation models have fundamentally transformed world modeling, shifting from traditional UNet backbones to Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures capable of capturing complex physical interactions and long-horizon consistency [4]. Systems such as Sora illustrate how massive video models learn implicit physical simulators from internet-scale data, though maintaining temporal consistency over extended horizons remains challenging [4].

To achieve interactivity, generative world models must condition their outputs on agent actions. DIAMOND demonstrated that diffusion world models can achieve top-tier performance in reinforcement learning benchmarks such as Atari 100k by capturing intricate visual details through action-conditioned generation [7]. Similarly, Genie introduced an 11-billion parameter foundation world model trained in an unsupervised manner from unlabelled internet videos without ground-truth action labels [3][8]. By combining a spatiotemporal video tokenizer, an autoregressive dynamics model, and a scalable latent action model (LAM), Genie infers discrete action tokens between frames and enables users or agents to step into and explore imagined virtual worlds [3][8]. 

Complementing pixel-space diffusion models, self-supervised representation learning approaches like V-JEPA 2.1 learn dense visual representations in abstract representation space rather than pixel space [5]. By leveraging dense predictive losses and multi-modal tokenizers, joint-embedding predictive architectures avoid modeling irrelevant high-frequency pixel noise while providing robust features for downstream action conditioning [5]. Earlier interactive architectures such as GameGAN also pioneered memory-module-based generative adversarial simulation for preserving spatial consistency without access to underlying game engines [9].

## Applications in Autonomous Driving and Robotics
World models serve as critical components in autonomous driving and embodied robotics, functioning as data engines, action planners, and generative simulators [10][11]. In autonomous driving, modern end-to-end paradigms integrate perception, prediction, and planning through structured representations and trajectory-level outputs [12]. Specialized benchmarks such as VehDyn evaluate whether generated driving futures obey realistic vehicle kinematics and dynamics rather than merely producing plausible visual aesthetics [10]. Similarly, nuTruck benchmarks planning for distributed electric-drive trucks, addressing heavy-duty vehicle dynamics largely unexplored by passenger vehicle datasets [13].

Behavior simulation benchmarks have also evolved to test reactive capabilities. ReactSim-Bench evaluates reactive behavior world model simulators where simulated agents respond dynamically to autonomous vehicle behaviors that deviate from standard logs [14]. Furthermore, platforms like WorldArena 2.0, World Reasoning Arena, and WorldModelBench provide comprehensive frameworks to assess action simulation fidelity, long-horizon forecasting, and instruction-following physics adherence [15][16][17]. In robotics, GigaWorld-1 and RoboVerse emphasize that long-horizon rollout consistency and robot-specific controllability are paramount for reliable policy evaluation and sim-to-real transfer [18][19].

## Evaluation, Sim-to-Real Transfer, and Reliability Gaps
Evaluating world models presents unique challenges, particularly distinguishing between open-loop visual realism and closed-loop control reliability. Recent comprehensive evaluations, such as WorldLens for driving world models and MiraBench for robotic world models, reveal critical discrepancies [20][21]. WorldLens demonstrates that high open-loop visual realism does not guarantee safe closed-loop control, as almost all existing generative world models trigger collisions or off-road drifts when deployed for trajectory planning [20].

Similarly, MiraBench uncovers pervasive optimism bias in robotic world models—a phenomenon where models possess strong priors toward task success and predict favorable outcomes even when conditioned on failure-inducing actions [21]. These findings establish that visual fidelity is a poor proxy for action-conditioned reliability [21]. Consequently, future benchmarking must prioritize action-following fidelity, physical constraint adherence, and robustness against compounding errors over superficial perceptual quality.

## Trends and Open Problems
The field of world models is rapidly advancing toward several key frontiers. First, scaling foundation world models from internet video to embodied interactive environments remains a primary engineering objective. Second, bridging the gap between open-loop generative fidelity and closed-loop control safety requires novel training objectives that penalize physical violations and action-following failures. Third, mitigating compounding errors in long-horizon rollouts necessitates advances in non-autoregressive diffusion frameworks, hybrid state-space models, and abstract latent representations. Addressing these open problems will determine whether world models can reliably underpin safe autonomous systems, advanced robotics, and general-purpose embodied AI.
